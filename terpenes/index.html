Terpenes

Terpenes are the aromatic compounds that give hemp its smell, from pine and citrus to earth and spice. Learning them helps you describe what you like and compare strains with more precision than family labels alone.

What terpenes do

Terpenes are made in the same resin glands as cannabinoids, and they shape aroma and flavor. They are also found in everyday plants: limonene in citrus peel, pinene in pine, linalool in lavender. In hemp, the mix of terpenes is part of what makes one strain smell and taste different from another.

The main terpenes

Myrcene is earthy and musky and is common across many strains. Limonene is citrusy and bright. Pinene smells of pine and fresh forest. Caryophyllene is peppery and spicy. Linalool is floral and soft. No single terpene decides a strain on its own; the full profile matters more than any one note.

Using terpenes to choose

When you find a strain you like, note its dominant aromas, then look for other strains with a similar profile. That approach beats chasing strain names, which vary between growers and regions. Strain guides and the strain library organize profiles by aroma for exactly this reason.
