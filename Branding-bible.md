# 1. 🌱 Identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | **Reefer Dude** |
| Short | ReeferDude |
| Domain | reeferdude.com |
| Mirror | potgrowhub.store |
| Slogan | **Hustle hard. Grow loud. Smoke smart.** |
| Second line | Two people, one truck, zero budget, all hustle. |
| Promise | Straight answers, honest gear, free tools. No flowery filler, no flower for sale. |
| Audience | Adults 21+, growers, DIY builders, head-shop regulars |
| Anti-audience | Corporate cannabis marketing, medical claim makers, hype merchants |

## 1.1 Brand Mission

Build a useful, honest, accessible digital workbench for cannabis education, cultivation knowledge, DIY projects, equipment research, creative experimentation, and responsible discovery.

Help visitors understand what they need, why it matters, what it costs, and what they can realistically do next.

## 1.2 Brand Vision

Create a recognizable, independent, resourceful brand that combines country roots, botanical curiosity, practical engineering, retro-futuristic design, and community-powered learning.

## 1.3 Brand Personality

- 🌲 **Grounded:** Practical, resourceful, and connected to the real world.
- 🌿 **Curious:** Always learning, testing, comparing, and improving.
- 🔧 **Capable:** Explains complicated things in understandable language.
- 🤝 **Human:** Friendly, respectful, and approachable.
- 🔥 **Bold:** Distinctive without becoming obnoxious.
- 🧠 **Honest:** Admits uncertainty and corrects mistakes.
- ♻️ **Resourceful:** Makes the most of available tools, time, and materials.

## 1.4 Brand Positioning

Reefer Dude is a practical cannabis-adjacent knowledge, DIY, and discovery brand for adults who want useful information without corporate gloss, manufactured hype, or unnecessary spending.

**Positioning statement:**

> For curious adults, growers, and hands-on builders who value useful knowledge and honest recommendations, Reefer Dude is the down-to-earth digital workbench that helps people learn, compare, build, and make better-informed decisions.

## 1.5 The Brand Promise

Every experience should deliver at least one of these outcomes:

- 📚 Learn something useful.
- 🛠️ Understand or build something.
- 🔍 Find and compare relevant resources.
- 💡 Turn an idea into a practical plan.
- 💸 Avoid wasting money.
- 🤝 Connect with a useful community or resource.

**The standard:** If a feature cannot explain its purpose or provide a genuine benefit, reconsider whether it belongs.

# 2. 🧬 Brand Architecture & Positioning

## 2.1 Brand Name Rules

- Use **Reefer Dude** in customer-facing copy.
- Use `ReeferDude` where a compact identifier is required.
- Use `reeferdude.com` as the primary domain when it is owned, configured, and verified.
- Treat `potgrowhub.store` as a related domain or mirror only when the ownership, routing, and brand relationship have been confirmed.
- Never imply that a domain, integration, partnership, or service is operational until verified.
- Do not introduce competing brand names into the experience without an explicit decision.

## 2.2 Brand Architecture

| Layer | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Brand | Identity, values, tone, visual standards |
| Website | Navigation, information, discovery, and user experience |
| Knowledge resources | Articles, guides, glossaries, references |
| Tools | Calculators, comparison aids, planning utilities |
| Community | Conversations, contributions, feedback |

1. Accuracy and user safety.
2. Honesty and transparency.

4. Useful information.

# 3. 🗣️ The Voice

Country boy. Hippie. Hustler. A little gangster swagger in the delivery, never in the ethics.

Warm, funny, direct, allergic to nonsense. Talks like a guy behind the counter who has heard it all, still gives a damn, and will tell you when the $200 rig is a waste of money.

## 3.1 Tone Dials

| Dial | Setting |
|---|---:|
| Warmth | 8/10 |
| Bluntness | 9/10 |
| Swagger | 7/10 |
| Slang | 6/10 — never at the cost of clarity |
| Hype | 1/10 |
| Medical claims | 0/10 |

## 3.2 Voice Formula

**Country-hearted + hippie-minded + street-smart + technically useful + unapologetically honest.**

The voice should sound like a real person helping another real person, not a marketing department trying to impersonate one.

## 3.3 Writing Rules

- Put the answer before the personality.
- Explain technical terms the first time they appear.
- Use slang sparingly enough that the meaning stays clear.
- Use contractions and natural phrasing.
- Prefer specific facts over superlatives.
- State prices, limitations, risks, and trade-offs plainly.
- Separate verified facts from opinions and estimates.
- Admit when information is unknown or outdated.
- Use humor to welcome people, never to humiliate them.
- Never shame someone for their budget, experience, mistakes, or questions.

## 3.4 Voice Examples

| Situation | Preferred expression |
|---|---|
| Practical recommendation | "Here's what works, what doesn't, and what I'd skip." |
| Budget limitation | "Let's make what you've got pull its weight." |
| Product comparison | "The expensive one ain't automatically the better one." |
| Uncertainty | "I can't verify that yet, so I'm not gonna sell you a guess." |
| Technical explanation | "Here's the plain-English version." |
| Error recovery | "Well, that didn't go to plan. Let's straighten it out." |
| Encouragement | "Start small, test it, and build from there." |
| Responsible caution | "Hold up a second. Here's what you need to know first." |

## 3.5 Never Sound Like

- A corporate press release.
- A fake country caricature.
- A medical professional when no such qualification exists.
- A financial or legal authority offering guaranteed outcomes.
- An aggressive salesperson.
- A bot manufacturing fake excitement.
- A social media account chasing controversy.

## 5.3 Semantic Colour Roles

| Role | Default token |
|---|---|
| Primary background | `--color-deep-resin` |
| Main text on dark | `--color-paper` |
| Supporting text on dark | `--color-smoke` |
| Primary action | `--color-ember` |
| Growth and success | `--color-bud` |
| Links and utilities | `--color-sky` |
| Structural borders | `--color-ink` |
| Secondary panels | `--color-forest` |
| Warm metallic accent | `--color-copper` |

Semantic colors communicate meaning consistently. A color must not mean "success" in one component and "danger" in another without a documented reason.

## 5.4 Colour Accessibility

- Verify actual text/background contrast for every combination.
- Target WCAG 2.2 AA contrast: 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text.
- Aim for 3:1 contrast for meaningful interface boundaries and graphical indicators.
- Never communicate errors, success, selection, or availability through color alone.
- Pair status colors with text, icons, or another perceivable signal.
- Test hover, focus, active, disabled, and selected states independently.
- Check transparency and overlays against the final rendered background.

# 6. 🔤 Typography

| Role | Family | Weight | Case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display | Archivo Black | 400 | UPPERCASE, tight tracking |
| Heading | Space Grotesk | 600–700 | Sentence case |
| Body | Space Grotesk | 400–500 | Sentence case, 18px/1.6 |
| Label | JetBrains Mono | 500 | UPPERCASE, wide tracking |
| Numerals | JetBrains Mono | 700 | Tabular for prices and specs |

## 6.1 Typographic Hierarchy

| Element | Suggested desktop size | Rules |
|---|---:|---|
| Display headline | 3–5rem | Bold, compact, controlled line length |
| H1 | 2.5–3.5rem | One clear page topic |
| H2 | 2–2.5rem | Major content section |
| H3 | 1.5–1.875rem | Component or subsection |
| H4 | 1.125–1.375rem | Supporting hierarchy |
| Body | 1.125rem | Comfortable reading |
| Supporting text | 0.875–1rem | Maintain adequate contrast |
| Eyebrow label | 0.75–0.875rem | Monospace, uppercase |
| Legal or metadata text | 0.875rem minimum where practical | Never hide important terms in tiny print |

## 6.2 Typography Rules

- Use one H1 per page as the default editorial convention.
- Maintain logical heading order.
- Do not choose heading levels for visual size alone.
- Avoid long uppercase paragraphs.
- Use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` for aligned numeric displays.
- Keep paragraphs at a comfortable reading width, generally 45–80 characters per line.
- Use fluid sizing only within defined minimum and maximum limits.
- Provide sensible system-font fallbacks if web fonts fail.
- Do not make the interface unusable when external fonts are blocked.

## 6.3 Font Loading

- Load only the font families and weights actually used.
- Prefer self-hosted, properly licensed font files when feasible.
- Use `font-display: swap` for suitable web-font implementations.
- Avoid loading large collections of unused font variants.
- Never allow a font-loading failure to conceal important content.

# 7. 🌿 Iconography & Emoji

Bud · Leaf · Bong · Pipe · Cartridge · Wax jar · Seed · Hemp stalk · Lighter · Grinder · Jar lid · Trimmer

**Style:** Single-stroke line icons at 2px, plus emoji glyphs for personality moments.

Every icon-only control carries an `aria-label`.

Buds and leaves mean growth and reward. Bongs and pipes mean gear. Cartridges and wax mean tools.

## 7.1 Icon Rules

- Use one consistent icon family wherever practical.
- Prefer 2px strokes for standard line icons.
- Keep decorative and functional icons visually distinct.
- Use recognizable symbols for common actions.
- Pair unfamiliar icons with visible text labels.
- Give icon-only buttons an accessible name.
- Hide redundant decorative icons from assistive technology.
- Never use an emoji as the only indicator of an important warning.
- Do not use emoji as a replacement for a properly labeled control.

## 7.2 Approved Icon Categories

| Category | Examples | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Botanical | 🌿 🍃 🌱 | Growth, learning, cultivation |
| Tools | 🔧 🛠️ ⚙️ | Building, setup, maintenance |
| Discovery | 🔍 🧭 ✨ | Search, exploration, ideas |
| Community | 🤝 💬 👥 | Conversation and collaboration |
| Safety | ⚠️ 🛡️ 🔒 | Caution, security, privacy |
| Success | ✅ 🎯 🌟 | Completed tasks and achievements |
| Commerce referral | 🛒 📦 ↗️ | Shopping research and external destinations |
| Technology | 💻 🧠 🛰️ | Software, AI, connected systems |
| Sustainability | ♻️ 🌎 🌳 | Reuse, conservation, environmental care |

## 7.3 Emoji Density

- Headlines: optional, generally one meaningful emoji.
- Buttons: optional, generally zero or one.
- Paragraphs: use sparingly.
- Warnings: text must remain explicit.
- Legal notices: avoid decorative emoji.
- Data tables: do not clutter cells with repeated decoration.
- Navigation: use icons only when they improve recognition.

## 7.4 Accessible Icon Example

```html
<button type="button" aria-label="Open search">
  <svg aria-hidden="true" viewBox="0 0 24 24">
    <!-- Insert the licensed or original search icon paths here. -->
  </svg>
</button>
```

Use real, correctly licensed icon artwork in production. The example intentionally leaves the icon path unspecified.

# 8. 🧱 Layout, Surface & Motion

- **Neo-brutalism** — 2px ink borders, hard 8px offset shadows, no blur on the frame.
- **Glassmorphism** — 14px backdrop blur, saturated panels, 9% white fill over Deep Resin.
- **Claymorphism** — inset double shadows, pill shapes, buttons you want to squeeze.
- **Retro-futurism** — mono labels, terminal spacing, orbital rotation.
- **Floating** — one slow float per screen, never two competing.
- **Orbital menu** — gear orbs circling a seed pod, 48px minimum targets.
- Motion: 160–250ms ease-out for interaction, 7s for ambient float, 26s for marquees.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` stops all of it.

## 8.1 Surface Hierarchy

| Surface | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Page canvas | Deep Resin or the selected theme background |
| Main content panel | Forest or Clay surface |
| Interactive card | Clear boundary and predictable hover treatment |
| Featured panel | Ember, Amber, or botanical accent used with restraint |
| Modal | High contrast, strong border, clear dismissal |
| Navigation | Stable position and distinct active state |
| Footer | Soil or Deep Resin with readable text |
| Utility overlay | Simple, legible, and easy to dismiss |

## 8.2 Border and Shadow Rules

- Default border: 2px solid a documented structural color.
- Use hard-offset shadows on prominent elements, not every element.
- Keep text shadows off body copy unless necessary for legibility.
- Do not use shadow alone to communicate component boundaries.
- Keep focus indicators visible even when a component has decorative borders.
- Avoid clipping focus rings with `overflow: hidden`.
- Do not make decorative outlines look like interactive controls.

## 8.3 Glassmorphism Rules

- Use backdrop blur selectively.
- Provide a readable opaque or sufficiently solid fallback.
- Keep important text legible over all underlying imagery.
- Do not put essential instructions exclusively on translucent surfaces.
- Avoid blur-heavy effects on low-powered devices.
- Respect the selected color theme.

## 8.4 Motion Rules

- Interaction transitions: 160–250ms.
- Ambient floating: approximately 7 seconds when enabled.
- Marquee loops: approximately 26 seconds when enabled.
- No rapid flashing, seizure-inducing effects, or forced movement.
- No essential information may depend on animation.
- Pause or remove nonessential animation when reduced motion is requested.
- Avoid scroll hijacking and automatic movement that prevents reading.
- Never animate the entire interface simultaneously.

# 9. 🖼️ Imagery & Art Direction

Hyper-realistic 3D render quality. Macro focus. God rays through smoke. Gloss meeting matte clay.

Palette: emerald, copper, amber, smoke grey. No smiling stock people. No text baked into images. No clip art. No watermarked supplier photos. Alt text describes object, place and mood.

## 9.1 Art Direction

Preferred visual subjects:

- Botanical macro photography.
- Grow equipment and tools.
- Reusable materials and DIY workspaces.
- Abstract cosmic environments.
- Retro-futuristic technical illustrations.
- Detailed product photography for legitimate external product research.
- Texture studies involving clay, metal, soil, glass, and leaves.
- Original diagrams and explanatory illustrations.

## 9.2 Image Standards

- Use assets that are owned, licensed, public domain, or otherwise permitted for the intended use.
- Do not remove watermarks or misrepresent image ownership.
- Do not imply that a pictured product is available from Reefer Dude unless that is true.
- Do not bake essential text into images.
- Generate suitable responsive sizes.
- Use modern formats where supported.
- Lazy-load noncritical images below the fold.
- Give meaningful images descriptive alternative text.
- Use empty `alt=""` for purely decorative images.
- Set image width and height or aspect ratio to prevent layout shifts.
- Never use misleading before-and-after imagery or fabricated testimonials.

## 9.3 Image Metadata

Where relevant, record:

- Asset filename.
- Title.
- Description.
- Creator.
- Source or origin.
- License and usage restrictions.
- Creation date.
- Dimensions.
- File format.
- Accessibility text.
- Associated page or component.
- Attribution requirements.

Image metadata must be accurate. It must not be used for keyword stuffing.

## 9.4 Image Naming Convention

```text
category-subject-context-size.ext
```

Examples:

```text
botanical-hemp-leaf-macro-1200.webp
tools-grow-tent-setup-800.webp
brand-reefer-dude-mark.svg
interface-search-icon.svg
```

These are naming examples, not claims that the assets already exist.

# 10. 🤝 Social & Community Voice

Reply fast, reply human, never reply from a script. Correct wrong information about us politely. Credit other builders by name. Never dunk on a competitor — out-teach them instead.

## 10.1 Community Principles

- Treat beginners with respect.
- Correct misinformation without humiliating the person who shared it.
- Credit original creators and contributors.
- Moderate threats, harassment, spam, and unlawful activity according to published rules.
- Never buy fake followers, fabricate community activity, or manufacture testimonials.
- Ask permission before publishing personal contributions.
- Do not expose private messages or personal information without authorization.
- Make community participation optional.
- Clearly distinguish staff, contributors, automated systems, and ordinary members.

## 10.2 Moderation Standard

Moderation should be consistent, explainable, proportionate, and appealable where practical.

When content is removed or an account action is taken:

1. Identify the applicable rule when safe to do so.
2. Explain the action in plain language.
3. Provide an appeal route when available.
4. Protect the privacy of involved individuals.
5. Do not promise an appeal process that has not been implemented.

# 11. ⚖️ Legal Guardrails

Never claim a health benefit. Never imply we sell flower or THC product. Never show consumption by anyone under 21. Always disclose affiliate relationships. Always show the 21+ gate before content. Alcohol-style responsibility, minus the alcohol.

## 11.1 Accuracy and Claims

- No unsupported medical, therapeutic, disease-treatment, or prevention claims.
- No guaranteed growing outcomes, product performance, savings, or income.
- No fabricated credentials, certifications, approvals, or legal opinions.
- No invented user reviews, usage statistics, partnerships, or testimonials.
- Distinguish educational information from individualized professional advice.
- Link to authoritative sources for material legal or safety claims where appropriate.
- Date information when regulations or product specifications can change.
- Correct known errors promptly.

## 11.2 Age Assurance

The brand's intended audience is adults aged 21 and older.

- Display the required age-entry experience before protected content.
- Use an explicit, understandable age confirmation.
- Do not imply that a self-declared age gate constitutes robust identity verification.
- Do not collect identity documents unless there is a lawful, necessary, and properly secured reason.
- Do not expose age-restricted material before the applicable restriction is satisfied.
- Avoid unnecessary storage of age-related data.
- Provide a clear exit route for visitors who do not meet the age requirement.

The precise legal requirements depend on jurisdiction, content, and business model. Obtain qualified legal guidance where needed.

## 11.3 Affiliate Disclosures

- Disclose financial relationships clearly and near the relevant recommendation.
- Explain when a qualifying purchase may generate a commission.
- Do not claim that an affiliate recommendation is independent if compensation materially affects it.
- Do not conceal disclosures in footers, tooltips, or hard-to-find legal pages.
- Never invent affiliate relationships or product availability.

## 11.4 Cannabis-Related Content

- Do not imply that cannabis is sold through the site unless a lawful, verified business model actually supports that statement.
- Respect applicable local, state, federal, and destination-market rules.
- Do not provide false assurances that an activity or product is legal.
- Avoid imagery that implies underage use.
- Distinguish educational discussion from product transactions.
- Use appropriate caution for pesticides, electrical systems, extraction equipment, and other potentially hazardous subjects.

# 12. 🏷️ Naming Conventions

Gear: plain name plus a vibe note. "Recycler rig — smooth, slow, built for sitting down".

Blueprints: place plus size. "Garage 8x10 — the two-light workhorse".

Strains: the grower names it, we only profile it.

Bonuses: "Smoke Break Bonus", never "loyalty points".

## 12.1 Interface Names

- Use recognizable words for navigation.
- Use descriptive titles for pages.
- Use consistent names for the same feature.
- Avoid multiple labels for identical actions.
- Do not rename standard accessibility controls merely to sound clever.
- Keep internal identifiers stable and machine-readable.

## 12.2 File and Code Names

- Prefer lowercase filenames.
- Use hyphens for multiword static assets.
- Use consistent naming conventions within each programming language.
- Keep public labels separate from internal identifiers.
- Avoid filenames containing dates or version numbers unless they are meaningful.
- Do not rename an existing file without accounting for references to it.

## 12.3 Example Naming Pattern

| Asset type | Example |
|---|---|
| Page | `grow-room-planning.html` |
| Stylesheet | `style.css` |

# 15. ♿ Accessibility

Use semantic HTML, visible focus styles, descriptive link labels, keyboard access, mobile layouts, alt text for meaningful images, and reduced-motion support. Never rely on color or emoji alone.

## 15.1 Standards

Target WCAG 2.2 AA as the accessibility baseline.

- Use semantic landmarks: `header`, `nav`, `main`, `aside`, and `footer` where appropriate.
- Use native HTML controls whenever possible.
- Keep all functionality available through a keyboard.
- Provide a visible, high-contrast focus indicator.
- Maintain logical focus order.
- Give every form field a programmatic label.
- Associate validation errors with their corresponding fields.
- Announce important dynamic status changes appropriately.
- Ensure dialogs manage focus and return it appropriately when closed.
- Respect browser zoom and text resizing.
- Support touch, keyboard, and assistive technology.
- Test with screen readers and automated accessibility tools.

## 15.2 Target Sizes

Aim for at least 44 × 44 CSS pixels for common touch targets.

WCAG 2.2 AA includes a 24 × 24 CSS pixel minimum target-size criterion with defined exceptions. The brand's 44 × 44 target is a more generous design goal, not a replacement for the applicable standard.

## 15.3 ARIA Rules

- Prefer native semantic HTML over unnecessary ARIA.
- Add ARIA attributes only when they convey meaningful information or behavior.
- Do not assign roles that contradict native element semantics.
- Ensure `aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`, `aria-pressed`, and other state attributes reflect the actual interface state.
- Do not use `aria-label` to conceal a missing visible label where a visible label is needed.
- Test accessible names and relationships after dynamic updates.

## 15.4 Reduced Motion

```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  *,
  *::before,
  *::after {
    animation-duration: 0.01ms !important;
    animation-iteration-count: 1 !important;
    scroll-behavior: auto !important;
    transition-duration: 0.01ms !important;
  }
}
```

This is a starting point. Review individual interactions to ensure that essential state changes remain perceivable and usable.

# 17. 🧩 Component Design System

Every reusable component should have a documented purpose, structure, interaction model, and accessibility behavior.

## 17.1 Buttons

Required variants:

- Primary.
- Secondary.
- Tertiary or text.
- Destructive.
- Icon-only.
- Disabled.
- Loading.

Rules:

- Use the correct HTML element for the intended action.
- Use a link for navigation and a button for an action.
- Provide a visible keyboard-focus state.
- Make disabled states understandable.
- Prevent repeated submission when a request is genuinely in progress.
- Display success only after confirmed completion.
- Provide an understandable failure state.
- Do not use a disabled appearance without actually disabling the control when appropriate.

## 17.2 Cards

Card types may include:

- Knowledge article.
- DIY project.
- Tool or calculator.
- External product reference.
- Community contribution.
- Informational warning.
- Featured guide.

Each card must provide a clear title and a useful next action when applicable.

Do not make an entire card appear clickable if only a small element is interactive. Avoid nested interactive elements that create confusing keyboard behavior.

## 17.3 Forms

- Label every field.
- Explain required fields.
- Identify invalid values in text, not color alone.
- Preserve entered values when safe after a recoverable error.
- Avoid asking for data that is not necessary.
- Explain why sensitive data is required before collecting it.
- Keep submission feedback close to the form.
- Provide a route to correct errors.
- Avoid clearing a completed form without an understandable reason.

## 17.4 Navigation

- Keep primary navigation consistent.
- Identify the current page or section.
- Use descriptive labels.
- Provide a skip-to-content link.
- Make mobile navigation keyboard accessible.
- Do not rely exclusively on an orbital menu or hover interaction.
- Provide a conventional fallback when a novel navigation pattern is introduced.

## 17.5 Search

A search interface should support:

- A visible label.
- Keyboard submission.
- Clear search behavior.
- Empty-query handling.
- Empty-result feedback.
- Loading feedback when appropriate.
- Recoverable errors.
- Relevant result titles and summaries.
- Optional filters when they materially improve discovery.
- A clear way to reset filters.

## 17.6 Dialogs and Overlays

- Use an accessible dialog pattern.
- Provide a clear title.
- Give users a visible dismissal method where dismissal is allowed.
- Keep keyboard focus within modal dialogs.
- Return focus to the invoking control after closing.
- Do not create unnecessary modal interruptions.
- Avoid trapping users behind a nonfunctional overlay.

## 17.7 Badges and Tags

Suggested labels:

- `FREE TOOL`
- `BEGINNER FRIENDLY`
- `DIY`
- `UPDATED`
- `EXTERNAL RESOURCE`
- `AFFILIATE LINK`
- `EXPERIMENTAL`
- `NOT YET AVAILABLE`
- `21+`

Rules:

- Only display a label when it is accurate.
- Do not use decorative badges for every item.
- Keep badge text legible.
- Never use a badge as the only indication of a legal restriction or safety warning.

## 17.8 Tooltips

- Use them for supplemental information, not essential instructions.
- Make relevant information available to keyboard and touch users.
- Avoid time-sensitive or difficult-to-trigger tooltips.
- Do not put legal disclosures exclusively inside a tooltip.

# 18. 🖱️ Interaction & Feedback States

Every interactive component must account for its relevant states.

| State | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| Default | Clear, predictable appearance |
| Hover | Optional pointer feedback |
| Focus | Visible keyboard-focus indicator |
| Active | Immediate indication of activation |
| Selected | Clearly distinguishable selected state |
| Disabled | Action unavailable and state understandable |
| Loading | Explain ongoing work where appropriate |
| Success | Confirm a verified successful result |
| Error | Explain what failed and the next step |
| Empty | Provide context and a useful next action |
| Offline | Explain network limitations when relevant |

## 18.1 Interaction Rules

- Do not trigger actions unexpectedly.
- Do not make users guess whether a click worked.
- Do not simulate successful payments, sign-ins, saves, or submissions.
- Do not hide important controls after activation without explanation.
- Do not use motion as the only confirmation.
- Make recoverable errors easy to correct.
- Preserve the user's work wherever safely possible.
- Make destructive actions explicit and reversible where practical.

## 18.2 Notifications

Notifications should be:

- Brief.
- Specific.
- Timely.
- Accessible.
- Nonblocking unless intervention is necessary.

Avoid excessive pop-ups, fake urgency, manipulative countdowns, and repeated prompts designed to wear down a user's refusal.

# 20. 🔎 SEO, AEO & Structured Data

Search Engine Optimization (SEO) helps search engines understand and discover content. Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) focuses on making useful answers understandable, extractable, and attributable.

Neither guarantees indexing, ranking, featured snippets, or AI-generated citations.

## 20.1 On-Page SEO

- Write a unique, descriptive title for each substantive page.
- Provide a clear meta description where useful.
- Use a descriptive, stable URL.
- Establish a logical H1/H2/H3 hierarchy.
- Write naturally for the intended reader.
- Link related pages using meaningful anchor text.
- Include descriptive image alt text.
- Use canonical URLs when there is a genuine canonical relationship.
- Avoid duplicate or thin pages.
- Keep robots directives aligned with intended indexing behavior.
- Maintain a valid XML sitemap when appropriate.
- Configure `robots.txt` correctly.
- Do not block resources required for search engines to understand the page.
- Use redirects carefully and preserve important existing URLs.

## 20.2 AEO Content Rules

- Answer the primary question early.
- Define technical terms clearly.
- Use descriptive headings.
- Present comparisons in readable tables where appropriate.
- Use ordered lists for procedures.
- Identify assumptions, units, dates, and limitations.
- Attribute important claims to reliable sources.
- Keep content coherent when a section is read independently.
- Do not publish fabricated FAQs to manipulate search results.
- Do not stuff pages with keywords, hidden text, or irrelevant synonyms.

## 20.3 Structured Data

Use Schema.org structured data only when the page genuinely represents the specified entity or content type.

Potential types, where eligible and accurate:

- `Organization`
- `WebSite`
- `WebPage`
- `Article`
- `BreadcrumbList`
- `Person`
- `Product`
- `ItemList`
- `HowTo`, only where supported and appropriate
- `FAQPage`, only where the implementation and current search-engine eligibility rules justify it

Rules:

- Do not mark up products that are not actually represented on the page.
- Do not invent prices, ratings, reviews, inventory, authors, or dates.
- Keep structured data consistent with visible page content.
- Use real URLs and identifiers.
- Validate generated JSON-LD.
- Update markup when the visible content changes.
- Do not assume that valid markup guarantees a rich result.

## 20.4 SEO Metadata Integrity

- Use only verified domain ownership and canonical relationships.
- Never invent search-engine verification tokens.
- Do not publish unverified organization details.
- Use the correct social preview image and metadata.
- Keep metadata descriptive, not promotional to the point of deception.
- Do not create misleading age, health, or product claims in search snippets.
